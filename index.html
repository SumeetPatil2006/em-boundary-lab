What is happening?

When an electromagnetic field reaches the boundary between two different materials, its components cannot change arbitrarily. Maxwell’s equations define the exact relationships governing the fields on either side of the interface.

Material 1 → Boundary → Material 2
1. Tangential field continuity

The tangential component of the electric field is strictly continuous across the interface: E₁ₜ = E₂ₜ (from Faraday's law ∮ E·dl = 0). Similarly, tangential magnetic field satisfies H₁ₜ = H₂ₜ when there is no surface current sheet (Jₛ = 0).

2. Normal flux density continuity

The normal component of electric flux density is continuous across an interface bearing no free surface charge (ρₛ = 0): D₁ₙ = D₂ₙ. Normal magnetic flux density is always continuous: B₁ₙ = B₂ₙ because isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist (∇·B = 0).

3. Field line refraction

Because D = εE, normal continuity requires ε₁E₁ₙ = ε₂E₂ₙ. Combined with E₁ₜ = E₂ₜ, this yields the law of refraction for electric field lines: tan(θ₁)/tan(θ₂) = εᵣ₁/εᵣ₂, where angles θ are measured from the interface normal.
